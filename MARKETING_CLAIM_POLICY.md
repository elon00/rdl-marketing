# Marketing Claim Policy

Last reviewed: 2026-09-22

## Principle

Every material public claim must be traceable to evidence that supports the exact wording.

## Evidence hierarchy

From strongest to weakest:

1. independently verifiable public-chain, deployment, customer, or third-party audit evidence;
2. reproducible repository tests and CI artifacts;
3. source-code implementation evidence;
4. internal benchmarks or repository-defined scorecards;
5. roadmap/design intent.

Do not promote a lower tier as if it were a higher tier.

## Required qualifiers

Use explicit qualifiers such as:

- local
- simulated
- prototype
- devnet
- testnet
- repository-tested
- internally benchmarked
- independently audited
- mainnet

Only use the qualifier actually supported.

## Financial and token claims

Do not promise or imply guaranteed returns, token appreciation, guaranteed staking rewards, exchange listings, liquidity, or investment outcomes.

Tokenomics documents are design/governance proposals unless deployment and governance evidence proves otherwise.

## Security and compliance claims

Do not use “audited”, “certified”, “FIPS validated”, “compliant”, “approved”, or similar terms unless the responsible independent body or audit evidence exists and is linked.

Using a standardized algorithm such as ML-DSA does not make an application a FIPS-validated cryptographic module.

## Performance claims

A performance claim must include, or link to:

- hardware/environment
- software version/commit
- workload
- sample size
- measurement method
- date
- limitations

Otherwise present it as a target or internal experiment, not a general capability.

## Partnerships and grants

“Applied”, “submitted”, “accepted”, “awarded”, “partnered”, and “sponsored” are distinct states. Never collapse them into one another.

## Review

Market-facing assets should be checked against this policy before publication.
